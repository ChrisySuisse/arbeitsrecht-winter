Workplace bullying is among the most difficult forms of conflict in an employment relationship. On the one hand, the term is sometimes used too readily today to describe any treatment perceived as unfair or hurtful. On the other hand, many workplaces still lack sufficient awareness of systematic exclusion, humiliation or harassment. Both tendencies make an objective assessment more difficult.

Not every conflict, unjustified criticism or isolated inappropriate remark constitutes workplace bullying in legal terms. The decisive question is whether hostile conduct is repeated over an extended period and, taken as a whole, is directed against a particular person. Such a situation may engage the employer’s duty of care and can have significant legal implications in connection with a termination, illness or other employment-related claims.

I have many years of experience advising on and litigating workplace bullying cases. I assist employees in establishing a clear record of events, preserving evidence and medical documentation, and realistically assessing whether the circumstances amount to workplace bullying in legal terms. Where legal claims exist, I represent clients in dealings and negotiations with their employer and, where necessary, before the courts. Equally, responsible legal advice requires a clear assessment where distressing or inappropriate conduct does not meet the legal requirements for workplace bullying.

Employers must take allegations of workplace bullying seriously and investigate them carefully. Where the necessary expertise is not available internally, or where personal or organisational connections prevent a sufficiently independent investigation, I conduct external investigations. These include reviewing relevant documentation, conducting structured interviews with the individuals involved and providing a clear and transparent assessment of the findings. The aim is to achieve a fair, confidential and robust clarification of the circumstances, providing a sound basis for appropriate measures within the organisation.
