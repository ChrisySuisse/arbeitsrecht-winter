The labour market in Northwestern Switzerland is strongly shaped by the tri-border region and cross-border cooperation. For many companies, employees residing in Germany or France are as much a part of everyday business as internationally recruited professionals. This creates particular legal requirements when structuring employment relationships.

Cross-border employment issues regularly involve employment law, tax law and social security law. Which rules apply depends, among other factors, on the employee’s place of residence, place of work, the extent of work performed abroad and nationality. An arrangement suitable for an employee residing in Germany therefore cannot simply be applied to an employee residing in France.

Home office arrangements require particular attention where part of the work is performed at the employee’s place of residence abroad. Issues that need to be addressed include the applicable social security system, taxation, withholding tax and the contractual definition of the place of work and applicable law. Further questions may arise in connection with employee assignments, notification requirements, accident insurance, family allowances and cross-border payroll.

Immigration law also plays an important role, particularly where companies wish to employ specialised professionals from non-EU/EFTA countries.
