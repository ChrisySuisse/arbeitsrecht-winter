Under Swiss employment law, the principle of freedom of termination generally applies: both employees and employers may terminate an open-ended employment relationship, provided that the contractual or statutory notice periods are observed. There is no general requirement to state a reason for termination. Nevertheless, the circumstances in which notice is given can be highly relevant from a legal perspective.

A termination may, in particular, be abusive if it is intended to prevent legitimate claims arising from the employment relationship or is given because an employee has asserted such claims. Terminations relating to an employee’s age, following many years of service, or arising from a conflict in which the employer may not have adequately fulfilled its duty of care also require careful assessment. The specific circumstances and events leading up to the termination are always crucial.

Particularly strict requirements apply to termination with immediate effect. Such termination is permissible only if it can no longer reasonably be expected that the employment relationship continue until the end of the ordinary notice period. In practice, terminations with immediate effect are often open to legal challenge because there is no sufficiently serious reason, a required prior warning was not given, or the employer waited too long before terminating the employment relationship.

If you have received notice of termination, I can assess its legality, review the circumstances leading up to it and advise you on the options available. This often involves more than compensation for abusive termination or unjustified termination with immediate effect. Other issues may include outstanding salary and bonus claims, unused holiday entitlement, overtime or social security matters.

Seeking legal advice at an early stage can help address the conflict appropriately, identify risks in good time and, where possible, prevent escalation. The effort and expense involved are often considerably lower than when legal advice is sought only at a later stage, once positions have hardened and the focus has shifted primarily to limiting legal and financial consequences.
