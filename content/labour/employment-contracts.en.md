Under Swiss law, employment contracts are generally not subject to any particular formal requirements. Nevertheless, it is advisable to set out the essential rights and obligations in writing, clearly and in a manner tailored to the specific employment relationship. Careful contract drafting creates certainty for both parties and provides a reliable basis if differing interpretations arise at a later stage.

Many employment law disputes can be traced back to unclear, contradictory or incomplete agreements. Anyone entering into a new employment relationship should therefore consider not only salary, position and working hours, but also provisions on bonuses, overtime, holidays, notice periods, non-compete clauses, place of work and home office arrangements.

I assist employees in understanding the legal implications of a proposed employment contract, identifying potential risks and assessing whether amendments would be advisable. For employers, I draft and revise employment contracts that reflect operational needs, clearly record the agreed terms and provide a sound basis should a dispute arise in the future.

A particular focus of my practice is on cross-border employment relationships, especially those involving cross-border commuters and home office arrangements with an international element. In these situations, employment law, tax law and social security law interact closely. I have worked on these interfaces for many years and have also published academic and professional articles on the subject. I advise you on developing contractual arrangements that are both legally sound and practical.
