Child protection matters are among the most emotionally challenging areas of family law. They concern the welfare and integrity of a child while often also affecting the fundamental relationship between parents and their children. The stakes are therefore particularly high.

The situation can become especially difficult when authorities become involved: following a report of a potential risk to a child, during investigations by the Child and Adult Protection Authority (KESB), or where the appointment of a deputy or more extensive child protection measures are being considered. In such situations, I consider it particularly important to seek legal advice at an early stage.

The best interests of the child are the central principle of child protection law. This does not, however, mean that the authorities have unlimited powers. Child protection measures must be based on a sufficiently specific risk to the child and must be proportionate. Where a less intrusive measure is sufficient, there should be no greater interference with family life and parental responsibility than necessary. Parents, too, are entitled to proceedings that comply with the rule of law and to have their position properly heard and carefully considered.

My experience shows that the quality of official investigations and the work of individual professionals involved can vary considerably. Authorities and appointed deputies can provide valuable support. However, there are also proceedings in which premature assessments, insufficient investigation of the facts or unprofessional communication further aggravate an already difficult family situation. A decision is not necessarily correct simply because it has been made by an authority.

I therefore assist you in dealings with the KESB and other authorities and professionals involved, attend meetings with you or represent you in such discussions, prepare written submissions, review measures that have been ordered and, where necessary, represent your interests in appeal proceedings.

When your own family is affected, it can be extremely difficult to be both emotionally involved and, at the same time, a composed participant in legal proceedings. I aim to take at least the legal and procedural burden off your shoulders. My role is not only to provide legally rigorous representation, but also to understand what is genuinely at stake for you and your child.
