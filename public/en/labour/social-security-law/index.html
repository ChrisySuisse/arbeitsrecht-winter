Social security law

Social security law issues are often closely connected with employment relationships. At the same time, social security law is a distinct and complex field in which employment law, insurance law and medical assessments intersect. I have dealt with specialised issues in this area since my university studies, subsequently in an academic context and, for several years, as part of my legal practice.

I represent clients in particular in disputes concerning daily sickness allowance benefits, questions relating to the legal assessment of incapacity for work and illness, and matters involving benefits under Swiss disability insurance. Other areas of my practice include occupational pension matters, such as changes of pension fund in connection with a transfer of business, as well as the assessment and enforcement of insurance claims.

A particular focus of my work is on disputes with daily sickness allowance insurers. Depending on the type of insurance arrangement, daily sickness allowance insurance is not social insurance in the strict sense but may instead be governed by private insurance law. Disputes frequently arise from differing assessments of an insured person’s capacity for work, reductions in benefits or the premature discontinuation of daily allowance payments. Such cases require a precise assessment of both the medical and legal circumstances as well as a clear negotiation strategy.

I handle correspondence and negotiations with insurers, assess medical opinions from a legal perspective and work to have unjustified reductions or discontinuations of benefits reversed. My experience from numerous cases shows that a carefully prepared case file and a consistent, persistent approach are often decisive. In many cases, this can result in a reassessment, the continuation of benefits or an appropriate settlement without the need for court proceedings. Where an out-of-court solution cannot be reached, I also represent clients in court.
